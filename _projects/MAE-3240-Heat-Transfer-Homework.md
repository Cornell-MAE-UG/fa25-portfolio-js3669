Working through this problem set strengthened my understanding of how heat transfer principles apply to real engineering systems. In Problem 1, deriving the governing differential equation for a rod with internal heat generation allowed me to truly understand the fundamental principles behind all the equations we use, and to develop physical intuition for what those equations represent. Problem 2 built on this by working backwards from measured temperatures to infer material properties, which felt much more like real engineering practice, where certain values can be directly measured and unknowns must be inferred through careful calculation.

Problem 3 allowed me to directly apply tools learned in class to a real-world engineering challenge: investigating the effect of a thermal barrier coating on a turbine blade. The comparison between the uncoated and TBC-coated cases made clear just how important these concepts are in practice - even an extremely thin ceramic layer can mean the difference between a blade being serviceable or not. This kind of analysis becomes especially valuable in design scenarios where there is no predetermined answer and the optimal material choice or coating thickness must be determined. Part of my internship this summer will involve electronic packaging, and developing a thorough understanding of heat transfer analysis through problems like these will prepare me well for that role.
